= EMES
:toc:

EMES (Executable Mechanical Engineering Specification) is an AI-native architecture for describing, generating, verifying, and iterating mechanical mechanisms as structured, version-controlled engineering data.

== Core idea

CAD is a derived artifact, not the source of truth.

[source,text]
----
requirements + design intent
          |
          v
 semantic mechanism IR
          |
    +-----+------+----------------+
    |            |                |
    v            v                v
  CAD         simulation       BOM / DFM
    \            |                /
     +-----------+---------------+
                 v
       deterministic evidence
                 |
                 v
        AI / optimizer patch
                 |
                 +------> repeat
----

The canonical source records requirements, topology, parameters, interfaces, load cases, objectives, constraints, and analysis intent. Generated CAD, simulation models, solver decks, reports, and BOMs remain reproducible projections.

A key architectural boundary is the separation between the semantic design graph and the evidence graph: AI may propose changes to the design, while registered tools and measurements produce the evidence used to accept or reject those changes.

Purchased components are also explicit engineering inputs. Motors, gearboxes, bearings, sensors, fasteners, battery cells, BMS units, power supplies, and converters can be normalized into versioned catalog snapshots containing dimensions, interfaces, unit-bearing properties, performance data, and attributed assets. A mechanism binds to a specific catalog/part digest rather than a live mutable vendor record.

== Current status

The architecture foundation and first external-tool vertical slice are merged:

[source,text]
----
EMES JSON
  -> semantic + catalog validation
  -> build123d -> exact BREP + STEP
  -> MJCF -> MuJoCo
  -> machine-readable evidence
----

The catalog layer supports deterministic import and pinned part binding. A synthetic motor fixture exercises the generic import contract, while `catalogs/maxon-496661.catalog.json` exercises the same architecture with an attributable real manufacturer motor snapshot. The maxon record is regenerated offline from a reviewed source snapshot and independently checked against hash-pinned manufacturer PDF/CAD assets.

The power-system layer adds composable electrical components. A catalog-backed 21700-format cell can be combined by explicit series/parallel counts into a battery pack, constrained by a BMS, passed through a DC/DC converter, and evaluated against electrical load, energy, current, voltage, and mass constraints. Fixed DC power supplies are also executable sources.

The first design-iteration loop is also executable on the synthetic power-pack example. `tools/power_pack_iteration.py` makes one allowlisted proposal (`P_PACK_PARALLEL: 2 -> 1`), validates and evaluates baseline and candidate through the same power evidence path, compares the declared pack-mass objective, and records a deterministic keep/reject decision. The lighter 10S1P candidate is rejected because it violates energy and current-margin constraints, demonstrating that objective improvement cannot override engineering acceptance.

All current evidence producers now share `spec/emes-evidence-v0.schema.json`: producer/runtime identity, role-labelled input digests, metrics, constraint results, and verification state use one validated envelope while geometry, battery, and other domain payloads remain domain-specific. Consumers validate the envelope and parent evidence digest before using upstream results.

`catalogs/molicel-p45b.catalog.json` is the first authoritative manufacturer battery-cell snapshot. It is normalized from the hash-pinned Molicel INR-21700-P45B Product Data Sheet v1.4, with an independently hash-pinned official performance page for discrete short-duration performance points. Manufacturer conditions remain attached to the engineering facts they qualify rather than being flattened into unconditional limits.

`examples/power-pack-molicel-p45b/mechanism.json` binds that real cell record to a hash-pinned JKBMS `JK-BD6A20S-6P` BMS and a manufacturer-backed SynQor `NQ60W60HGC40NRF-G` buck/boost converter configured for a 36 V output. The power evaluator accepts the P45B continuous-discharge rating only when the mechanism explicitly selects the source-backed 70 degC JKBMS factory-default discharge over-temperature setting. It also checks the BMS 7--20S and 20--100 V operating envelope against the full modeled pack range. This is executable sizing/traceability evidence, not a thermal model, certification, or permission to fabricate or energize a battery pack.

The same P45B example has a separate source-conditioned battery-sag analysis. `tools/battery_sag.py` reuses the power evidence instead of recomputing the load path, applies the P45B typical DC-impedance value only at its stated 50% SOC condition, and derives first-order series/parallel pack resistance and resistive voltage drop. Unsupported SOC values are rejected rather than interpolated. The adapter deliberately does not infer loaded terminal voltage without an attributable OCV-vs-SOC model.

`tools/battery_pulse.py` consumes exact manufacturer short-duration points through a structured analysis request. The current P45B catalog exposes 168 W/cell at 10 s and 50% SOC and 184 W/cell at 10 s and 90% SOC. An unsupported SOC or duration is rejected rather than interpolated or extrapolated.

`tools/battery_known_envelope.py` combines the cell pulse envelope with known downstream component limits using dimensionless load-scale factors while preserving each rating's native unit. Pack-side current limits use the maximum current demand over the modeled battery voltage range rather than the nominal-voltage current. For the reference unfused 10S2P/500 W case, the SynQor 40 A input-current limit is the weakest currently modeled native limit at a 1.9 load-scale factor, corresponding to a 950 W output envelope. The 95% converter efficiency used to derive battery-input current remains an explicit EMES analysis assumption rather than a manufacturer-guaranteed operating-point value. The NQ60 examples now also declare a requirement-level 1500 uF / 60 mOhm / 63 V input-damping network. `tools/power.py` fails closed below the manufacturer 1500 uF minimum, at or below the 50 mOhm ESR threshold, or below the full 42 V pack maximum. Passing those checks records only the datasheet minimums; it is not a COTS capacitor selection or whole-system stability certification.

`examples/power-pack-molicel-p45b-fused/mechanism.json` adds a first-class series fuse using reviewed manufacturer data for Littelfuse `4998040.M-M6` (40 A, 70 V DC). The evaluator requires fuse DC voltage rating to cover maximum pack voltage and includes nominal fuse current as a conservative known-limit cap without calling it a 10-second pulse rating. A 15 A counterexample moves the weakest-link identity to the fuse; a 30 V counterexample is rejected against the 42 V maximum pack voltage. The upstream Littelfuse PDF remains explicitly `raw_bytes_not_pinned` because automated source acquisition is blocked, so this is not physical safety approval. Interconnect/conductor thermal ampacity, thermal enclosure behavior, imbalance, aging, charger behavior, and certification limits remain incomplete.

The same fuse snapshot keeps its manufacturer `38 A at 20 degC` maximum-allowed-current point separate from the 40 A nominal rating. `examples/power-pack-molicel-p45b-fused/known-envelope-20c.json` explicitly requests that source condition; only an exact 20 degC context adds the 38 A candidate. Unsupported temperatures are rejected rather than interpolated, extrapolated, or silently substituted with nominal current.

`examples/power-pack-molicel-p45b-connected/mechanism.json` adds reviewed pack-side hardware: Littelfuse `DCNHF60NG12-F` contactor, Southwire stock `569973` 6 AWG two-conductor SOOW cable, and an Anderson Power Products SB50 assembly using 992G4 housings and 5900 high-force contacts. `tools/battery_connector_envelope.py` composes their native limits with the parent weakest-known evidence and now fails closed on a directly connected conductor/contact wire-size mismatch. Southwire publishes 55 A allowable ampacity for that exact cable construction, while Anderson specifies contact 5900 for 6 AWG and provides a 6 AWG / 50 A / 23 degC-rise reference point. The conductor scale is 2.6125, SB50 is 2.375, contactor is 2.85, and the upstream SynQor/fuse envelope remains limiting at 1.9 / 950 W. A synthetic 10 AWG direct-termination counterexample is rejected instead of silently applying the 5900 rating; reducer-bushing derating, routing, bundling, terminal/crimp process quality, busbars, and enclosure thermal behavior remain outside automatic approval.

A custom EMES language remains intentionally deferred until concrete examples prove it is necessary.

== Local acceptance

After installing `requirements-dev.txt`, run the same normal acceptance suite used by GitHub Actions:

[source,shell]
----
bash tools/ci/validate.sh
----

The focused battery/provider workflows likewise invoke repository-owned scripts under `tools/ci/`; workflow YAML only installs dependencies and dispatches those checks.
