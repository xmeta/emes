= Electrical Power and Battery Composition

== Scope

EMES treats electrical power hardware as engineering components that participate in the same catalog, provenance, connection, analysis, and evidence model as mechanical parts.

Phase 1 adds catalog kinds for `battery_cell`, `battery_pack`, `bms`, `power_supply`, `power_converter`, `charger`, `fuse`, `contactor`, and `connector`.

The first battery example uses a synthetic 21700-format Li-ion cell fixture. Its values validate the architecture only and must not be interpreted as a real cell recommendation. A second synthetic fixture demonstrates a catalog-backed fixed 48 V DC power supply.

The namespaced `org.emes.power` structure has its own JSON Schema at `spec/emes-power-v0.schema.json`; it is not an untyped metadata convention.

== Cell versus pack

A repeated cell is stored once as a catalog-backed component. A battery pack refers to that cell plus integer series and parallel counts.

For a cell with nominal voltage `V_cell`, capacity `C_cell`, continuous current `I_cell`, and mass `m_cell`:

[stem]
++++
V_{pack} = N_s V_{cell}
++++

[stem]
++++
C_{pack} = N_p C_{cell}
++++

[stem]
++++
E_{pack} = V_{pack} C_{pack}
++++

[stem]
++++
I_{cells} = N_p I_{cell}
++++

[stem]
++++
m_{cells} = N_s N_p m_{cell}
++++

This representation lets an AI vary `N_s` and `N_p` as design variables without duplicating every physical cell in the semantic graph.

== Protection limits

Cell multiplication never bypasses downstream limits. For the Phase-1 BMS model:

[stem]
++++
I_{pack,continuous} = min(N_p I_{cell}, I_{BMS})
++++

The evaluator also rejects a pack if its series count exceeds the BMS series rating or if its maximum charge voltage exceeds the BMS pack-voltage rating.

A DC/DC converter is rejected if the complete pack voltage interval falls outside the converter input interval. Output-power and pack-current margins remain executable metrics and constraints.

== Battery power path

The reference battery topology is:

[source,text]
----
catalog-backed 21700 cell model
          |
       Ns x Np
          v
     battery pack
          |
        BMS
          |
          v
     DC/DC converter
          |
          v
      DC load bus
----

`extensions.org.emes.power` records pack composition and the power path. Ordinary EMES `components`, `connections`, and `functional_edges` still describe the design graph.

== Fixed power supply

A fixed supply is a normal catalog-backed `power_supply` part with output voltage, continuous current, continuous power, mass, and electrical interfaces. The reference fixture is connected directly to a DC load:

[source,text]
----
catalog-backed power_supply
          |
          v
       DC load
----

The Phase-1 evaluator takes the lower of the declared power rating and `V * I` as the usable continuous source-power limit. It reports bus voltage, load current, source-current margin, and source-power margin. CI requires a 500 W load to fail against the 48 V / 10 A / 480 W fixture.

== Evidence

`tools/power.py` produces deterministic machine-readable evidence containing source design digest, selected catalog identities and digests, power topology, derived electrical metrics, and executable constraint results.

For battery paths this includes series/parallel topology, voltage range, capacity, nominal energy, BMS-clamped current capability, cell/pack mass, converter margin, and equivalent pack current. For fixed supplies it includes bus voltage, source current/power capability, and load margins.

CI also mutates the valid battery fixture to 13S and requires rejection by the 12S BMS limit, and separately overloads the DC/DC converter. These expected failures verify that protection and rating constraints are executable rather than documentation-only.

== Safety and fidelity boundary

The Phase-1 model is an architectural/electrical-sizing model, not a battery safety certification model. It does not yet model state of charge, voltage sag, cell imbalance, temperature, aging, internal short circuits, propagation, charging profiles, enclosure venting, fuse coordination, or regulatory compliance.

Physical pack construction, charging, and actuation require later safety evidence and explicit human approval. A passing Phase-1 power analysis must never be interpreted as permission to fabricate or energize a battery pack.
