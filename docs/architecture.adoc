= EMES Architecture
:toc:
:toclevels: 3

== 1. Purpose

EMES (Executable Mechanical Engineering Specification) is an AI-native engineering architecture for developing physical mechanisms from version-controlled, machine-readable specifications.

The central design choice is that CAD is *not* the source of truth.
The source of truth is a semantic engineering model that records requirements, design intent, mechanism topology, physical parameters, interfaces, load cases, objectives, constraints, analysis plans, and traceability.
CAD, simulation models, meshes, BOMs, drawings, and verification reports are compiled or measured artifacts.

The Phase 1 target is the smallest useful closed loop:

[source,text]
----
human goal
   |
   v
EMES source
   |
   v
parse + normalize + semantic checks
   |
   v
Canonical Mechanism IR
   |
   +--> geometry adapter --> CAD / STEP
   +--> dynamics adapter --> MJCF / SDF / Modelica
   +--> analysis adapter --> FEA / kinematics / collision
   +--> manufacturing adapter --> BOM / process checks
   |
   v
deterministic verification + metrics
   |
   v
evidence bundle
   |
   v
AI proposes a source patch
   |
   +----------------------------> repeat
----

== 2. Design principles

=== 2.1 Semantic source before geometry

A solid model answers "what shape is it?" but usually does not fully answer:

* why a component exists;
* which function it performs;
* what it connects to;
* which requirement it satisfies;
* which loads it must survive;
* which variables may change;
* which trade-offs are intentional;
* what evidence justifies a performance claim.

EMES therefore keeps geometry as one projection of a richer design model.

=== 2.2 Deterministic engineering core, probabilistic proposal layer

LLMs may generate hypotheses and patches.
They must not be the authority that decides whether a design passes.

The compiler, dimensional checks, geometric checks, simulation adapters, verification rules, and evidence aggregation are deterministic or explicitly stochastic with fixed seeds and recorded uncertainty.

This separation prevents an AI from "arguing" a failed design into acceptance.

=== 2.3 Explicit units and coordinate frames

Every dimensional quantity must carry a unit at authoring boundaries.
Canonical normalization uses SI units internally.
Every pose, vector, joint axis, load, and inertia tensor must identify its reference frame.

Implicit unit conventions are forbidden at the canonical IR boundary.

=== 2.4 Stable identity

Requirements, parameters, components, connections, load cases, metrics, and verification rules receive stable IDs.

Stable IDs allow:

* precise diffs;
* requirement-to-evidence traceability;
* cross-backend mapping;
* AI patch targeting;
* experiment comparison;
* stale-evidence invalidation.

=== 2.5 Source and derived artifacts are separated

AI agents modify source specifications and generator code.
They do not edit generated STEP, meshes, solver decks, or verification summaries by hand.

Generated artifacts are reproducible products associated with a source digest and a toolchain digest.

=== 2.6 Fidelity is explicit

A result is never just "verified".
Each claim records the method and fidelity used to support it, for example:

* analytic;
* kinematic simulation;
* rigid-body dynamics;
* low-fidelity FEA;
* high-fidelity FEA;
* prototype measurement.

Higher-fidelity evidence may supersede lower-fidelity evidence, but the history remains attributable.

=== 2.7 Backend neutrality

EMES is not a new geometry kernel, physics engine, optimizer, or CAD application.
It is a semantic IR, compiler/orchestrator, evidence model, and AI design loop that can target existing tools.

== 3. Layered model

EMES uses six logical layers.

=== L0 -- Mission and requirements

Defines what the mechanism must accomplish.

Examples:

* payload >= 5 kg;
* reach >= 700 mm;
* total mass <= 8 kg;
* backlash <= 0.2 deg;
* parts cost <= JPY 100,000;
* no collision throughout a specified trajectory.

Requirements are immutable statements during an optimization run unless a human explicitly changes the problem definition.

=== L1 -- Design intent and functional graph

Represents why the design has its structure.

The functional graph may express relations such as:

[source,text]
----
motor_j2
  --transmits_rotational_power-->
timing_belt_j2
  --drives-->
joint_j2

motor_j2
  --located_on-->
base
  --because-->
reduce_moving_inertia
----

Design intent is advisory unless protected by a hard requirement.
An AI may challenge design intent, but the change is explicit and reviewable.

=== L2 -- Mechanism topology

Represents physical entities and their relationships independently of detailed geometry.

Core entity classes:

* body / link;
* joint;
* actuator;
* transmission;
* spring / damper;
* bearing;
* fastener;
* sensor;
* cable / hose;
* structural member;
* interface;
* material assignment;
* purchased part.

Connections form a typed graph.
A serial robot, four-bar linkage, belt transmission, differential, compliant mechanism, or vehicle suspension can therefore be represented before exact CAD exists.

=== L3 -- Parametric realization

Binds topology to concrete design variables and geometry generators.

Examples:

* link length;
* tube outer diameter and wall thickness;
* bearing series;
* pulley tooth count;
* motor SKU;
* joint offset;
* plate thickness;
* hole pattern.

Geometry may be generated by adapters such as build123d, CadQuery, KCL, FeatureScript, FreeCAD Python, or OpenSCAD.
The geometry program is a backend artifact or referenced source module, not the universal EMES semantics.

=== L4 -- Analysis and manufacturing models

Defines analysis projections and load cases.

Potential adapters include:

* analytic equations;
* kinematics;
* collision checking;
* multibody dynamics;
* Modelica multi-domain simulation;
* MJCF / MuJoCo;
* SDF / Gazebo;
* FEA;
* tolerance stack-up;
* thermal analysis;
* cost and BOM;
* manufacturability checks.

The same design can have several analysis models at different fidelities.

=== L5 -- Evidence and decision record

Every evaluated metric is stored with provenance:

[source,text]
----
metric
  value
  unit
  requirement/objective IDs
  source design digest
  adapter + version
  solver settings
  input artifact digests
  stochastic seed (if any)
  uncertainty / tolerance
  timestamp
----

The result of an AI design iteration is therefore not only a CAD model; it is a design plus a machine-verifiable evidence bundle.

== 4. Canonical representation

=== 4.1 Authoring form versus canonical IR

Phase 1 intentionally avoids inventing a custom programming language.

The first canonical interchange representation is JSON validated by JSON Schema.
Future authoring frontends may include YAML, TOML, a dedicated DSL, GUI editing, or natural language, but all frontends lower into the same typed canonical IR.

Reasons:

* JSON has mature parsers in nearly every language;
* JSON Schema provides an executable structural contract;
* canonical JSON is straightforward to hash;
* the project can validate semantics before committing to language syntax.

A dedicated DSL should only be introduced if actual usage demonstrates that JSON/YAML plus code modules is insufficient.

=== 4.2 Canonicalization

Before compilation, an EMES implementation performs:

. schema validation;
. unique-ID validation;
. reference resolution;
. dimensional normalization to SI;
. coordinate-frame resolution;
. topology consistency checks;
. design-variable bound checks;
. requirement and metric linkage checks;
. deterministic ordering for hashing.

The normalized document receives a `design_digest`.

=== 4.3 Extension mechanism

Unknown ad-hoc fields in the core namespace should be rejected.
Experimental semantics live under namespaced extensions:

[source,json]
----
{
  "extensions": {
    "org.example.flexure": {
      "model": "pseudo-rigid-body",
      "parameter": 0.85
    }
  }
}
----

An extension cannot silently alter the meaning of a core field.

=== 5.1 Adapter contract

Every adapter receives:

* canonical IR subset;
* explicit adapter configuration;
* dependency/tool versions;
* deterministic seed when relevant.

Every adapter returns:

* generated artifact references;
* metric records;
* diagnostics;
* provenance;
* success/failure status.

An adapter must not mutate canonical IR.

=== 5.2 Artifact graph

Derived files form a content-addressed dependency graph.

For example:

[source,text]
----
mechanism.json
  -> normalized-ir.json
      -> arm.step
      -> arm.mjcf
      -> static-load.inp
          -> static-load.result
              -> metric:max_stress
              -> metric:tip_deflection
----

When an upstream digest changes, dependent evidence becomes stale automatically.

== 6. Requirements, constraints, and objectives

EMES distinguishes four concepts.

`requirement`::
Human-facing statement of required behavior or property.

`constraint`::
Executable pass/fail condition over a metric.

`objective`::
Quantity to minimize or maximize when more than one passing design is possible.

`design variable`::
A parameter the optimizer or AI is permitted to change.

Example:

[source,text]
----
REQ-PAYLOAD: "Carry at least 5 kg at full extension."

load case LC-FULL:
    payload = 5 kg

constraint C-STRESS:
    metric max_von_mises_stress
    <= allowable_stress / safety_factor

objective O-MASS:
    minimize total_mass
----

This makes "goal" and "means" distinct.
The optimizer may discover a different mechanism while preserving the requirement.

== 7. AI design loop

The AI layer operates as a proposal engine, not an oracle.

=== 7.1 Iteration

. Read requirements, current source, design intent, evidence, and diagnostics.
. Identify the binding constraint or highest-value objective.
. Propose the smallest coherent source patch.
. Compile and validate.
. Run the cheapest analysis capable of falsifying the proposal.
. If it passes, run the next required verification level.
. Compare evidence against the current baseline.
. Keep or reject the candidate.
. Record the decision and repeat until a stopping condition is met.

=== 7.2 Search modes

The proposer interface can support several strategies without changing EMES semantics:

* LLM patch generation;
* parameter sweep;
* Bayesian optimization;
* gradient-based optimization;
* evolutionary search;
* graph-grammar topology search;
* mixed strategies.

This is important because no single optimizer is appropriate for continuous dimensions, discrete catalog choices, and topology changes simultaneously.

=== 7.3 Cost-aware verification ladder

A candidate should encounter cheap falsifiers first:

. schema and semantic checks;
. analytic bounds;
. geometry regeneration;
. kinematics and collision;
. rigid-body dynamics;
. coarse structural analysis;
. higher-fidelity multiphysics;
. physical prototype measurement.

This minimizes wasted simulation cost.

=== 7.4 Anti-reward-hacking boundary

The proposer cannot modify:

* acceptance criteria for the active run;
* verifier implementation;
* reference datasets;
* protected safety factors;
* measurement data.

Such changes require a separate human-reviewed change to the problem definition or toolchain.

== 8. Interoperability strategy

EMES should reuse existing ecosystems instead of replacing them.

=== Geometry

Preferred adapter candidates:

* build123d;
* CadQuery;
* KCL/Zoo where cloud/API use is acceptable;
* FreeCAD Python;
* OpenSCAD for simple constructive geometry.

STEP is an exchange artifact, not the authoring source.

=== System dynamics

* Modelica for multi-domain acausal physical models;
* MJCF/MuJoCo for fast robot dynamics;
* SDFormat for simulation interchange;
* URDF for ROS ecosystem compatibility.

URDF, SDF, and MJCF are outputs/projections because they do not encode the complete engineering intent of a general mechanism.

=== Optimization

EMES can expose its analyses and metrics to:

* OpenMDAO;
* GEMSEO;
* specialized optimizers.

Phase 1 does not embed one optimization framework into the core.

=== Co-simulation

FMI is a suitable interoperability boundary for exchanging dynamic models and co-simulation components.

== 10. Minimal Phase 1 acceptance path

The first executable vertical slice is intentionally narrow:

[source,text]
----
two-link arm JSON
    -> JSON Schema validation
    -> semantic reference checks
    -> canonical digest
    -> pass/fail requirement linkage report
----

The next implementation slice should add exactly one geometry adapter and one dynamics adapter, rather than implementing all backends at once.

Recommended order:

. build123d or CadQuery geometry generation;
. MJCF export and MuJoCo kinematic/dynamic smoke test;
. BOM/cost adapter;
. one structural analysis path;
. AI patch/evaluate loop.

== 11. What EMES is not

EMES is not:

* a replacement for a BREP geometry kernel;
* a replacement for Modelica;
* a replacement for SysML;
* a universal solver;
* an LLM-only design system;
* a binary CAD file versioning scheme;
* a promise that simulation equals manufacturability.

Its role is to connect engineering semantics, executable toolchains, verification evidence, and AI-driven iteration through a stable source model.

== 12. Safety boundary

Any transition from digital design to physical fabrication or actuation must be an explicit gate.

For safety-critical or high-energy mechanisms, simulation success is not sufficient authorization for fabrication or operation.
Required human review, standards compliance, material certification, safety factors, and physical validation belong in project-specific verification policy.

== 13. Phase 2 candidates

The following are deliberately outside the first-complete architecture:

* custom EMES DSL;
* graph-grammar topology synthesis;
* differentiable co-design;
* uncertainty propagation;
* tolerance and reliability optimization;
* supplier catalog integration;
* automated DFM/DFA;
* electrical/hydraulic/control co-design;
* formal SysML v2 import/export;
* FMI co-simulation orchestration;
* physical test ingestion;
* learned surrogate models;
* distributed simulation scheduling.

They should be added only after a concrete end-to-end design exposes the need.
